Washday: lo-fi prototype

Washday helps residents of a building with a shared laundry room book a washer and a dryer back to back, and agree on what happens when laundry is left in a machine. These wireframes come from the Week 3 and 4 context mapping. The vibe-coded build follows them.

Open the build →Codebase on GitHub

Problem statement

Residents of a building with a shared laundry room spend long, unpredictable stretches of time waiting for occupied machines and for other residents to remove finished laundry. When a machine is busy, the wait often pushes their laundry into late-night hours, and they cannot plan their day around it.

Design goal

Residents can wash and dry their clothes at a time they have planned, without waiting for machines or for other residents to clear them.

From findings to design decisions

FindingDesign decision
1A wash takes about 1.5 h and a dry 2–2.25 h. Without a slot for both, one participant finished at 4 am.Resident interviewBook a wash + dry as one load. The app finds a washer and a dryer that are free back to back and shows when you'll be done. Each slot is as long as the real cycle, not a fixed hour.
2When nothing is free, people wait or book a slot "late into the night".Resident interviewThe home screen shows the next free wash + dry. If a day is full, the app points to the earliest free time. Cycles must end by 22:00 (quiet hours).
3The real pain is other people's finished laundry. The resident nudges owners in the group chat and sometimes threatens to take the clothes out.Resident interviewA Pickup watch with graduated steps: 15 min to collect, then a ready-made nudge, then the basket after 30 min.
4Shared resources work when there are clear rules, mutual monitoring and graduated sanctions. House rules already allow moving clothes to a basket.Ostrom (1990), Germany Handbook: HausordnungRoom rules, written down and visible next to the schedule, so nobody has to guess the unwritten ones (the original brief).
5Apps that notify residents when a cycle ends are spreading. My assumption about forgetting was not confirmed in the interview.Property Manager Insider · Resident interviewA lightweight calendar reminder (.ics) for the start and end of your cycle, without push infrastructure. Low priority until further research confirms it.
6Walk-ins sometimes grab a free machine on the spot.Resident interviewYou can still book the slot that is starting now, so walk-ins stay visible to others.

Main flow

Open app→See "next free wash + dry"→Plan a load→Booked + reminder→Cycle ends → collect→Not collected? Nudge → basket

Wireframes

Open questions for the next iteration

  • Only one resident was interviewed. Do residents who forget their own laundry exist, and would they use the reminder?
  • Shared, real-time data: the build keeps bookings on one device (demo). A real building needs a shared backend and sign-in per apartment.
  • Should a resident who uses the basket often lose booking priority? That would be a graduated sanction, but it is risky socially.
  • Fixed Waschplan (German buildings) vs. free booking: support both?
Lo-fi wireframes are intentionally rough: grayscale, hand-drawn type, placeholder lines. The visual design lives in the build.
